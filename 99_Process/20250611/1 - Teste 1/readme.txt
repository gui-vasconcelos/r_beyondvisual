Prompt for Image 1: Terracotta Courtyard (30-Second Version)
Soundscape Title: Terracotta Transfiguration (A 30-Second Contemplation)
Overall Arc: The piece is a single, slow breath: a gentle swell and a long, peaceful exhale, contained within 30 seconds.
[0s - 5s] Emergence: The piece opens with the sound already present, not fading in from silence. A solo cello drone (warm, flautando bowing, no vibrato) is immediately audible, drenched in a vast, diffuse convolution reverb. This establishes the immense, warm space from the very first second. The sound has a subtle analog tape saturation.
[5s - 20s] Blooming: At the 5-second mark, an ethereal synthetic string pad (VST-style, simple consonant chord) begins to gently swell in from the background. It has a very slow attack. Its volume peaks gracefully around the 18-second mark, representing the height of the contemplative state, but it never overpowers the cello.
[20s - 30s] Resolution: From 20 seconds, the string pad begins a slow fade to silence. This leaves the solo cello drone exposed once more for a few seconds before it too recedes, allowing the final 3-4 seconds of the track to be nothing but the long, warm tail of the reverb resolving into silence at the 30-second mark.
Final Instructions: Absolutely no percussion. The dynamic movement is crucial: a slow bloom and an even slower decay. The piece should feel like a brief, fleeting moment of profound peace captured in time.
Prompt for Image 2: Concrete and Moss Hall (30-Second Version)
Soundscape Title: Geometry of Silence (A 30-Second Imposition)
Overall Arc: The structure is one of oppressive quiet, a sharp punctuation, a climactic rupture, and a cold, lingering decay.
[0s - 8s] The Weight of Space: The piece begins abruptly with the established atmosphere. A deep, cold wavetable synthesizer drone with a sub-harmonic frequency is present, alongside a very quiet, underlying texture of subtle granular noise (dry foliage texture). The entire sound is defined by a Large Hall Reverb with sharp, clear early reflections. This 8-second introduction establishes the cold, immense, and tense environment.
[8s] The Punctuation: Exactly at the 8-second mark, a single, dry, percussive impulse (like a processed double bass pizzicato) strikes. It is a sharp, loud sound. Its immediate echo ricocheting through the hall reverb is the primary focus for the next few seconds.
[18s] The Rupture: At 18 seconds, a forceful, atonal, dissonant piano cluster is struck (fortissimo). This is the climactic event. Its long, clanging, metallic decay becomes the primary sonic element for the remainder of the piece, resonating powerfully within the hall's acoustic space.
[18s - 30s] The Cold Decay: For the final 12 seconds, the drone and granular noise remain as a low foundation while the piano's dissonance slowly decays. The piece does not resolve. It ends abruptly at 30 seconds, perhaps cutting off the very last whisper of the reverb tail, leaving a sense of unease.
